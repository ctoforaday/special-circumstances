# The Catechism

Adapted from the Heilmeier Catechism — reshaped from a funding pitch into a **worth-our-time
decision**. We are not asking anyone for money; we are deciding whether to spend limited time and
attention. Answer in plain language, as the *agreed* post-debate positions — no jargon, no
enthusiasm standing in for evidence.

1. **What are we trying to do?** (No jargon.)
2. **How is it handled today, and what does that cost us?** The limits of current practice, in our
   context specifically.
3. **What is new here, and why do we believe it works?** Evidence, not enthusiasm.
4. **The case against.** Every honest reason NOT to do this — cost, complexity, risk, maintenance
   burden, opportunity cost — each stated at full strength, not as a strawman. The idea must
   survive its strongest objections; an idea that has run out of honest objections is a candidate
   for a good one.
5. **Of interest, or merely interesting?** Given limited time, what would doing this displace, and
   why does it beat what it displaces? Half of all questions are interesting; far fewer are of
   interest. If we cannot yet tell, say so and name what we would need to learn first.
6. **What changes if it works — and what happens if we simply don't do it?** Both sides of the
   ledger; "nothing bad happens if we skip it" is a verdict.
7. **What does it cost, and where would we stop?** Time, effort, and complexity budget, plus the
   checkpoints at which we would abandon it rather than sunk-cost onward.
