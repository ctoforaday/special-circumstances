## menu

change the report — the only path into report.md, and how every repair lands

## detail

A raw write or a shell edit of the report is denied.

TAKING RED'S FIX AS IT STANDS. Where red prescribed concrete replacement text on a gap, you can
apply that text instead of retyping it. Your argument for the change is still required.

Retyping it is the same act only if every byte matches: a stray space or a re-wrapped line records
that you did NOT take red's text, and costs you the estoppel that stops red re-opening its own words.

You are never obliged to accept. Writing your own replacement is your right to disagree and is
recorded as one.

The tool matches your span across the invisible anchor layer and REFUSES a replacement that drops, duplicates or invents an anchor: an anchor of any kind may travel THROUGH your edit, never disappear in one.

YOUR QUOTE DECIDES WHICH ANCHORS THE EDIT TOUCHES. An anchor your quote does not carry is outside the span and stays exactly where it stands. That covers the commonest place for one — the anchors at the END of a sentence, before its terminator: quote the sentence without them, write the replacement without them, and they stand after your new text, before the terminator, untouched. Leave the closing punctuation off that replacement — the terminator already stands after the anchors — because a replacement that ends its own sentence there is refused: it would leave them on that sentence, whichever sentence held the claim each marks. Typing one of them into the replacement without quoting it is refused, and the refusal names it.

An anchor your quote carries, at its start or its end, or one in the middle of the quoted text, is INSIDE the span and must be copied verbatim into the replacement, where its claim now stands. That is how an anchor at a sentence's end MOVES — onto an earlier sentence of a longer replacement, say: quote the sentence as `show report` prints it, anchors included, and write each one where it goes. Quoting one anchor of a run takes every anchor between it and your quoted text into the span with it.

A RUN AT A SENTENCE'S END DOES NOT SAY WHICH PART OF THE SENTENCE EACH ANCHOR MARKS. When your replacement holds more sentences than the text it replaces, write each anchor your quote carries after the sentence that now holds its claim. A replacement that carries two or more anchors from the end of that text to the end of its own last sentence is refused, and the refusal prints what the record says each one marks beside your sentences, numbered. Where every one of them does belong on the last sentence, make the change in two edits: reword that sentence, its anchors kept, in one, and add the other sentences in a second that keeps it word for word.

Either way the record lists each anchor whose sentence your edit changed. A citation or proof among them is reopened: its owning lens owes a check of it as it now stands and is readied for one, a check by any lens from a sitting opened after your edit clears it, and a PASS waits until one does.

Quote enough context that the span matches exactly ONE place, or the edit is refused as ambiguous rather than applied to the wrong site.

REPAIRING PUNCTUATION. Because of that trim, a repair confined to the trailing punctuation — dropping a stray period, changing a terminator — would land on the span without it. When the trimmed span would change nothing, or would leave the old terminator standing beside the new one, the tool uses your quote exactly as written instead, punctuation included — provided it occurs byte-for-byte exactly once in the report. So quote the punctuation as the report prints it, anchors and spacing included; this works at the end of the document too.

AN EDIT THAT CHANGES NOTHING IS REFUSED, and nothing is recorded: the refusal says whether the punctuation trim was the cause. The same holds when taking red's fix — a prescription that would leave the report as it is is refused as changing nothing, which is different from a prescription the report has moved away from.

NO SECTION THE TOOL COMPOSES. Assembly composes these itself, from the record: {{.RefusedHeadings}}. An edit that adds one of them to the report as a `## ` heading, or an `**Outcome:**` line above the first heading, is refused and nothing is recorded; a heading the edit completes from parts is one it adds. The refusal names the section, what assembly composes it from, and how content reaches it: the act that puts such content on the record, or that no act of yours does and whose does. {{.SubjectHeadingsNote}} `show report` has an assembled form that gives the same for every section before you write.

A SECTION MOVES IN ONE EDIT. Quote the section together with everything that stands between it and its place, as `show report` prints them, and write the same text as the replacement with the section where it belongs: every anchor in the span stays on its sentence and no sentence changes, so nothing is reopened. Taking a section out in one edit to write it back in a second is refused when the section holds an anchor, because the first edit would drop it.

WHAT MAY BE IN THE REPORT: research prose for a reader of the SUBJECT, and the markers this tool places — nothing else. No provenance or attribution tags, no notes to another seat, no argument about the run, no narration of how the report was made. A fact that LIMITS THE CONCLUSION stays, re-voiced as a limit on the answer rather than a story about the attempt. Operational facts go to `log`, arguments to `closing`, or to `position` where your seat files one.
