## menu

attach a source to a sentence, when a claim needs evidence a reader can follow to the leaf

## detail

The tool fetches and caches the source and splices an invisible anchor at the sentence — you never type a footnote. It survives every later edit: `edit` refuses a replacement that drops one.

To RESOLVE an anchor you meet while reading (not create one), read the `evidence` projection, which lists every source by anchor id.

THE TITLE IS PRINTED IN THE REPORT, as the source's Bibliography entry. Give the source's own name — author, work, section — for a reader of the SUBJECT, not an account of how you found or read it: no "this sitting" or "this run", no "re-read at the leaf". How much of the source you read is recorded as its own field, not in the title's words. Where that reading matters to a claim, say it in the report's prose, in the subject's terms: "the source, read in full, states…", "the abstract claims, and the body does not show, …". The tool flags a title that sounds like the run talking about itself; it refuses nothing, because a real source's own title can.
