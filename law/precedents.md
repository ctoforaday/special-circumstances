## apparatus-count-cadence [AFFIRMED 2026-09-07]
facts: On the 2026-09-02 quadratic-formula run, blue's report stated "Executing all fourteen
  stored scripts ... nine reproduce byte-identically ... while five do not". By the time the bench
  ran that loop itself the store held EIGHTEEN scripts, THIRTEEN reproducing — and the five that
  did not were the same five, by name, with the same exit statuses. The denominator had moved
  because blue deposited round-2 proofs while repairing the sentence that counts them. The same
  mechanism appears twice more on that record: the lines-of-inquiry denominator ran 23, then 26,
  then 31 across three rounds, and blue's own repair note observes that correcting 23 to 26 "would
  have shipped the same defect with a fresher number".
question: Is a count over the run's own apparatus, correct when written and overtaken by the same
  round's later work, a fresh instance of the figure-recount defect class?
holding: No. A count the report states over the run's OWN APPARATUS — programs written, proofs
  deposited, lines of inquiry filed, edits recorded — that was CORRECT WHEN WRITTEN and has since
  been overtaken by the same round's later work is a STALENESS ARTIFACT OF THE MEASURING CADENCE,
  and is read rather than docked. It is a defect only if (a) it was wrong when written, or (b) the
  report presents it as current at a site where a reader acts on it and the drift changes what the
  reader would do.
rationale: Under any other reading the class mints one gap per round FOREVER, because the act of
  measuring the apparatus is itself an act that changes the apparatus — the round that repairs the
  count creates the next instance. That is not convergence but a defect generator with a period of
  one round, and it is precisely the case in which a further judged round has negative yield. The
  value in tension is accuracy of the shipped artifact, and it is protected by the two exceptions
  rather than surrendered: a count wrong when written is still a defect, and so is one a reader
  would act on differently.
scope-limits: Assumes the denominator is INTERNAL to the run. A count over an external artifact —
  cached bytes, a source document, a published paper — has a denominator that does not move when
  the run measures it, and staleness there is an ordinary defect. Does not reach a count that was
  wrong when written. Does not reach a site where the drift changes a reader's action. Distinguish
  wherever the count could be GENERATED rather than authored: see cadence-exemption-ends-at-
  generation, which narrows this holding on exactly that ground.
source: 2026-09-02_quadratic-formula, round 2, holding filed beside R2-2

## cadence-exemption-ends-at-generation [AFFIRMED 2026-09-07]
facts: In round 3 of the same run both parties invoked the round-2 apparatus-cadence holding, in
  opposite directions, and both were right about their own instance. The round-2 holding had named
  generation as "the durable fix" and then asserted, as a fact about the world, that nothing in
  either seat's surface could do it — an assertion the round-3 record contradicted.
question: How far does the apparatus-count cadence exemption reach?
holding: The cadence exemption is available only where an apparatus count is hand-authored BECAUSE
  NOTHING CAN GENERATE IT. Where a seat can deposit a program that walks the directories and checks
  the reconciliation, the exemption does not reach it, and staleness there is an ordinary
  figure-recount defect. A seat that CAN generate a count and re-authors it by hand instead has
  chosen the failure mode.
rationale: The earlier holding existed to stop a defect generator with a period of one round. The
  right end state is not a standing exemption but the disappearance of the generator, so the
  exemption must retreat as generation becomes possible — otherwise it ossifies into an amnesty for
  hand-authored numbers and rewards the seat that declines to automate. This also records a
  precedent-versus-leaf conflict resolved the way the statute requires: the round-2 holding's
  factual premise was contradicted at the leaf, and the leaf won.
scope-limits: Narrows apparatus-count-cadence and disturbs nothing else in it. Assumes the
  generating program is within the seat's actual surface, not merely imaginable; a count nobody can
  generate today keeps the exemption until someone can.
source: 2026-09-02_quadratic-formula, round 3, holding filed beside R3-2

## withholding-needs-both-limbs [AFFIRMED 2026-09-07]
facts: R2-5 was withheld from the bench's docket as "minted fresh this round, so not a persisting
  dispute". The record showed both sides had argued it to closing — red in its round-2 closing
  narrative, blue at length under its own heading in its round-2 position. The same withholding
  then misfired twice more in the same run: R3-1, which red made the headline of its round-3 filing
  and blue the headline of its answer, and R3-4, which red raised against a defect its own seat had
  caused and blue answered under its own heading with a control and a filed line of inquiry.
question: When may a freshly minted gap be withheld from the bench's docket as "not a persisting
  dispute"?
holding: Only when BOTH limbs hold: freshly minted AND not argued by both sides. A gap both parties
  argued to closing is a persisting dispute in everything but mint round, and is docketed.
rationale: An argued gap that reaches no ruling returns to red's verdict pool as though nobody had
  considered it — so red weighs, against certification, a gap whose acceptance check passes. In the
  measured cases that would have returned the round's most consequential finding to the pool with a
  PASSING check. Lineage is a proxy for persistence and a poor one; what the rule is actually for is
  keeping the bench off disputes nobody is having, and two seats arguing to closing is the direct
  evidence the proxy was standing in for.
scope-limits: Assumes "argued by both sides" means argued ON THE RECORD to closing — a filing and an
  answer, not a mention. Does not license docketing a gap neither party has engaged. Does not reach
  withholding on any other ground.
source: 2026-09-02_quadratic-formula, round 2 (R2-5), applied again at R3-1 and R3-4

## complexity-prices-the-required-fix [AFFIRMED 2026-09-19]
facts: On 2026-08-23_research-loop-counterparts, R4-3's `required_fix` asked for a textual change —
  name the axis, state current control coverage. Red graded complexity `medium` while pricing a
  mitigation the required_fix never asked for (building an egress policy), conceding in the same
  mint_reason that "the ask here is textual". Two sibling gaps at the same site cluster, R4-1 and
  R4-2, carried the same class of textual fix and were graded `low`.
question: Does the complexity axis price the repair the gap actually requires, or the fuller
  mitigation a reader might imagine the defect deserves?
holding: Complexity prices THE REQUIRED FIX AS WRITTEN. The axis is what fixing THIS gap costs — the
  work named in `required_fix` — and not the cost of a hypothetical remedy the gap did not ask for.
  A grade that prices an unrequested mitigation is the same category error whichever axis it lands
  on, and it is visible as an internal inconsistency whenever sibling gaps whose fixes are the same
  KIND of work carry different grades.
rationale: The required_fix is the gap's own statement of what would satisfy it, and blue is held to
  exactly that. Grading against a larger remedy charges blue for work nobody may demand and nobody
  will check, which detaches the grade from the only artifact that can settle it. It also makes the
  grade unfalsifiable: a reader cannot tell an expensive fix from an expansive imagination.
scope-limits: Assumes the required_fix is well-formed — a fix that understates the work is a defect
  in the MINT, argued as such, not a licence to grade the imagined version. Does not reach
  likelihood or impact, which price the defect rather than the repair. Does not bar raising the
  complexity grade where the stated fix genuinely costs more than it appears; it bars pricing work
  the fix does not require.
source: 2026-08-23_research-loop-counterparts, motion M2 at R4-3, never ruled before the ceiling

## ceiling-names-the-stop-not-the-board [AFFIRMED 2026-09-19]
facts: 2026-08-23_research-loop-counterparts reached assembly with the board fully closed — the
  bench itself closed the final gaps at its terminal sitting, substituting for a re-audit the
  ceiling foreclosed. Red's last verdict was FAIL, and red never saw the repairs. The CEILING enum's
  own text reads "the round ceiling was reached with work still open".
question: Is a CEILING stamp available when the board is clean at the moment of stamping?
holding: CEILING NAMES WHAT STOPPED THE DEBATE, NOT THE BOARD'S STATE WHEN IT STOPPED. The enum's
  text describes the typical case; it is not a precondition. A run halted by its limit is a CEILING
  run whether the board is full or empty, because the test for VERIFIED is not "is the board clean"
  but "did red affirmatively say so". A bench closing gaps in red's stead is not red speaking,
  however correct its re-verification.
rationale: Reading the enum text as a precondition forces a choice between two false stamps — a
  VERIFIED nobody affirmed, or no stamp at all for a state the record plainly reached. Both discard
  the distinction the verdict exists to carry: who ended the run, and on whose authority.
scope-limits: Binds how CEILING is read against a cleared board. Does not reach HALTED, which
  outranks it, nor a run that ends before any terminal state. Says nothing about whether the bench
  SHOULD close gaps in red's stead — only how the resulting stamp is read.
source: 2026-08-23_research-loop-counterparts, terminal bench sitting

## open-at-a-ceiling-stop-is-not-carried [AFFIRMED 2026-09-19]
facts: On 2026-08-23_sleeper-service-plan, two gaps stood `open` when the ceiling stopped the run.
  Neither had closings filed, and the terminal bench sitting ruled on four other gaps without
  reaching them. Blue's repairs had landed edits that, on direct reading, satisfied red's stated
  required_fix.
question: May a later reader treat a gap left `open` at a ceiling stop as though the bench had
  `carried` it?
holding: NO. `carried` IS AN AFFIRMATIVE BENCH JUDGMENT — reached after reading a docketed item's
  filed closings, that the gap survives with a stated research direction the next seat owes. A gap
  the bench never reached is `open` and nothing more. The two are not interchangeable, and a reader
  who conflates them credits the record with a decision nobody made.
rationale: `carried` carries an obligation forward; `open` carries only a fact. Treating the second
  as the first invents a research direction no seat owes and no bench stated, and it launders the
  ceiling's interruption into the appearance of deliberate continuation.
scope-limits: Assumes the absence of a ruling is visible on the record — no closings, no disposition.
  Does not reach a gap the bench ruled and chose to carry. Does not imply an unreached gap is
  unimportant: it implies only that its disposition is UNSTATED.
source: 2026-08-23_sleeper-service-plan, R4-1 and R4-5 at the ceiling stop

## a-repair-answers-the-reading-it-refutes [AFFIRMED 2026-09-19]
facts: On 2026-09-02_quadratic-formula, an evidence reading recorded `refutes` against a report
  clause that was subsequently docketed and REPAIRED. The shipped report then agreed with the
  reading, but the terminal PASS gate still fired: it keys on the raw evidence reading, and the
  surface carries no verb to answer or retract one.
question: How is a `refutes` reading answered once the text it refuted has been repaired away?
holding: THE REPAIR ANSWERS THE READING, AND THE LINEAGE-SUCCESSOR GAP IS THE READING'S BOARD HOME.
  A reading that refutes text which no longer exists is not a live contradiction; it is a finding
  the record already acted on. It is answered by minting a lineage successor that supersedes the
  repaired gap and closing it against the repair, which gives the reading a disposition the gate can
  see. The reading itself is never rewritten — it was true when taken.
rationale: The gate is right to refuse a PASS over an unanswered refutation, and wrong only about
  what counts as an answer. Without a lifecycle the reading can attach to, a correct repair produces
  a permanent block, which teaches seats to avoid recording refutations — the opposite of what the
  evidence layer is for.
scope-limits: Assumes the repair actually satisfies the reading, verified against the shipped text
  rather than asserted. Does not reach a reading whose claim survives in the report. Does not license
  superseding a gap to clear a gate where no repair happened; the successor must close ON the repair.
  Narrowed the moment the surface grows a verb to answer a reading directly — at that point the verb
  is the answer and this construction is spent.
source: 2026-09-02_quadratic-formula, red-lens-r3-L6 against anchor c-4e4c92af
